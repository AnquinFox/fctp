# Wise & Revolut in China: The Smart Way to Pay Without Getting Ripped Off 💸

> 3% foreign transaction fee × 20 transactions = ¥400 in waste. Here's how to keep that money.

---

## The Problem Nobody Warns You About

Here's the thing about China that catches most first-timers off guard: **every time you use your normal foreign credit card here, you're getting hit with fees.**

Your bank charges 3%. Visa/Mastercard adds their own spread. Alipay tacks on another 3% for foreign card usage. By the end of a two-week trip, you've probably paid ¥300–600 in fees that you never saw coming.

I learned this the hard way. On my first trip I used my standard Chase Visa for everything through Alipay. After two weeks I checked the statements — over ¥800 in fees and exchange rate markups. Never again.

That's where **Wise** and **Revolut** come in. These are the two cards I now use exclusively for China, and they'll save you real money.

## Card #1: Wise (Formerly TransferWise)

![Phone showing mobile payment app at a store counter](/images/general/payment-mobile.webp)

**Wise is my go-to card for China.** Period. Here's why.

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **What Is It** | Multi-currency account + debit card. Think of it as a bank account that lives in your phone. |
| **Key Feature** | Real exchange rate (no markup). Whatever Google shows is what you get. |
| **Cards Issued** | Visa debit (physical + digital) |
| **Set-up Time** | 10 minutes on their app |
| **Delivery Time** | 5-10 days for physical card |
| **Countries** | Available in 170+ countries |

### Why Wise Wins for China

**The exchange rate is the star here.** Wise uses the mid-market rate — the same one you see on Google or XE.com. No hidden spread, no markup. When the market says ¥7.24 per USD, you get ¥7.24 per USD.

Compare that to:
- Your bank's rate: probably ¥7.05 (you lose ¥0.19)
- Airport exchange counter: ¥6.80 (you lose ¥0.44)
- ATMs with foreign card fee: ¥6.95 equivalent after fees

Over ¥10,000 in spending, the difference between Wise and a normal bank card is roughly ¥250–400. That's a decent dinner.

### How to Use Wise in China

**Step 1 — Set Up Before You Go**

1. Download the Wise app on your phone (do this in your home country)
2. Create an account — passport/ID needed for verification
3. Fund your account via bank transfer, debit card, or wire
4. Order the physical card (free or small fee depending on region)
5. Add the digital card to Apple Pay / Google Pay immediately

> ⚠️ Set everything up before departure. Chinese app stores don't have Wise. The Chinese internet also blocks parts of the Wise website.

**Step 2 — Add to Alipay**

1. Open Alipay → **Me** → **Bank Cards**
2. Tap **"+"** to add a card
3. Enter your Wise card details (16-digit number, expiry, CVV)
4. Alipay does a micro-charge of ¥1–10 to verify
5. Check the Wise app for the verification code
6. Enter the code in Alipay
7. Done — your Wise balance is now spendable through Alipay

**Step 3 — Add to WeChat Pay**

1. WeChat → **Me** → **Services** → **Wallet** → **Cards**
2. Tap **Add a Card**
3. Enter Wise card details
4. Verify via SMS / Wise app
5. Ready to use

## Card #2: Revolut

| **Key Feature** | Free currency exchange on weekdays, budgeting tools, disposable virtual cards |
| **Cards Issued** | Visa/Mastercard (physical, virtual, and disposable) |
| **Set-up Time** | 10 minutes |
| **Delivery Time** | 5-7 days for physical card |
| **Plans** | Free (Standard), Premium ($10/mo), Metal ($17/mo), Ultra ($55/mo) |

### Where Revolut Beats Wise

| Feature | Revolut | Wise | Verdict |
|---------|:-------:|:----:|:-------:|
| **Virtual cards** | ✅ Yes, disposable virtual cards | ❌ Physical + digital only | Revolut 🔥 |
| **Weekend exchange** | 1% fee on weekends | Same mid-market rate always | Wise 🔥 |
| **Apple Pay / Google Pay** | ✅ Yes | ✅ Yes | Tie |
| **Crypto** | ✅ Buy/sell crypto | ❌ No | Revolut 🔥 |
| **Insurance** | On Premium+ plans | ❌ No | Revolut 🔥 |
| **Exchange rate** | Mid-market (weekdays) | Mid-market (always) | Wise 🔥 for weekends |
| **Best for China** | Good | Better | Wise wins by a nose |

### Setting Up Revolut for China

**Step 1 — Before You Go**

1. Download Revolut app (do this at home, same issue with Chinese app stores)
2. Verify identity with passport
3. Top up your account (bank transfer or debit card)
4. Create at least one virtual card specifically for China
5. Enable contactless payment
6. Subscribe to the right plan — Standard is enough for most, Premium worth it if you stay longer than 2 weeks (med insurance included)

**Step 2 — Add to Alipay & WeChat Pay**

Same process as Wise:

For Alipay:
1. Alipay → **Me** → **Bank Cards** → **Add**
2. Enter Revolut card details
3. Complete micro-charge verification from Revolut app
4. Done

For WeChat Pay:
1. WeChat → **Services** → **Wallet** → **Add Card**
2. Enter Revolut details
3. Verify
4. Ready

> ⚠️ **Important note on Revolut in China**: Revolut's fraud detection occasionally flags Chinese transactions. Before using Revolut in China, set your location in the app to "China" and enable international usage. If a transaction fails, open the Revolut app — you'll usually see an "Approve this transaction?" prompt within 30 seconds.

### Revolut's Disposable Virtual Card Trick

This is something Wise can't do: **Revolut lets you create disposable virtual cards.** These are single-use cards that self-destruct after one transaction.

**Why this matters for China:**
- Some Chinese platforms (especially when booking trains on 12306, or hotels on Ctrip) have dodgy payment gateways
- Use a disposable card for these → if the details get stolen, they're useless after one use
- Create a new virtual card for every online transaction
- Zero risk of card cloning

## Head-to-Head: Which One for China?

![Paying at a vendor using mobile phone](/images/general/payment-vendor.webp)

I've used both extensively in China. Here's the straight talk.

### For Your Average Tourist Trip (1-3 Weeks)

| Factor | Winner | Why |
|--------|:------:|-----|
| **Exchange rate** | 🏆 **Wise** | Same great rate 24/7, including weekends |
| **Ease of setup** | 🏆 **Wise** | Simpler app, fewer steps to link to Alipay |
| **ATM access** | 🏆 **Wise** | ¥200 free/month vs Revolut's ~$35 |
| **Virtual cards** | 🏆 **Revolut** | Disposable cards = peace of mind |
| **Apple Pay** | Tie | Both work seamlessly |
| **App reliability** | 🏆 **Wise** | Fewer false fraud flags in China |
| **Insurance** | 🏆 **Revolut** | Travel insurance on Premium |
| **Best value** | 🏆 **Wise** | Lower overall fees for the average trip |

**My verdict for a 2-week trip: Use Wise.**

Here's why: for the majority of short-term visitors, you won't need Revolut's extra features (disposable cards, crypto, insurance). What you will need is reliable payments with no surprise FX fees. Wise nails that.

### For Digital Nomads, Expats, and Long-Stays

| Factor | Winner | Why |
|--------|:------:|-----|
| **Monthly fee** | 🏆 **Revolut** | Premium ($10) includes insurance, pays for itself |
| **Multi-currency** | Tie | Both hold 30+ currencies |
| **Virtual cards** | 🏆 **Revolut** | Disposable = game changer for Chinese platforms |
| **Long-term fees** | 🏆 **Revolut** | Free ¥70k/month FX on Standard |
| **Business features** | 🏆 **Revolut** | Better business accounts |
| **Recurring payments** | 🏆 **Wise** | Direct debits are simpler |

**My verdict for longer stays: Use both.** That's what I do. Wise as primary (Revolut triggers false fraud flags on me about once a week in China). Revolut as backup with disposable cards for online purchases.

### The Budget Hack: Stack Them

**Here's the power move:** link *both* Wise and Revolut to Alipay and WeChat Pay. Then:
- Use **Wise** for daily spending (street food, metro, convenience stores)
- Use **Revolut virtual card** for online bookings (hotels, train tickets, DiDi rides)
- Keep your normal bank card as last resort

This way you get the best of both worlds and virtually eliminate FX fees.

## Real Talk: What Actually Goes Wrong

![WeChat app interface on a phone screen](/images/general/apps-wechat.webp)

After four trips using Wise and Revolut in China, here's what actually happens on the ground:

### Wise Problems I've Encountered

**"Transaction declined at a small shop"**
- This happened to me once at a street stall in Chengdu
- The vendor's QR code was linked to a personal Alipay account, not a merchant account
- Solution: switch to WeChat Pay — it went through immediately
- Wise works with 99% of merchants. The 1% is usually tiny street vendors.

**"My physical Wise card was declined at an ATM"**
- Some Chinese ATMs, especially older ones in smaller cities, don't support Wise's card network
- Solution: use Alipay to transfer money to a Chinese friend and have them withdraw cash
- Or find a Bank of China or HSBC ATM — those work best with foreign cards

**"The Wise app won't open in China"**
- Parts of Wise's website are blocked on China's internet
- The app itself works fine on mobile data (your roaming or eSIM)
- On WiFi (hotel, cafe), it may be slow or refuse to load
- Solution: use mobile data for Wise app, or connect a VPN

### Revolut Problems I've Encountered

**"Revolut transaction flagged as suspicious"**
- This happens. A lot. Revolut's fraud detection is aggressive with Chinese merchants.
- Solution: keep the Revolut app open when making purchases. Within 30 seconds you'll get a push notification asking "Approve this transaction?"
- Tap approve and it goes through
- Annoying but not blocking

**"Weekend exchange rate"**
- Revolut's free FX is only on weekdays
- If you transfer money on a Saturday, Revolut adds a 1% weekend markup
- Solution: top up your Revolut account before the weekend. Or use Wise for weekend reloads.

**"Revolut card not adding to certain Chinese apps"**
- Some smaller Chinese apps (Didi, Meituan) don't accept Revolut cards when adding as a payment method
- Solution: add Revolut through Alipay instead (Alipay acts as a middle layer)

### What Hardly Ever Goes Wrong

**Alipay + Wise:** This combo has been rock solid for me across 4 cities and ~150 transactions. Only 2 issues total.

**WeChat Pay + Wise:** Also very reliable. Maybe 1 issue in ~100 transactions.

**Both apps on Apple Pay / Google Pay:** Works everywhere the merchant accepts contactless. Tap and go.

## FAQ: What I Actually Get Asked

**Q: Do I need both Wise AND Revolut, or is one enough?**
A: For a 2-week trip, just Wise. For longer stays or if you're doing lots of online bookings, grab Revolut too as backup. Both are free to set up, so there's no reason not to.

**Q: Can I link my Wise/Revolut card to Apple Pay for use in China?**
A: Yes — both support Apple Pay and Google Pay. The acceptance of Apple Pay in China is decent at major retailers, but QR code payment (Alipay/WeChat) is still king.

**Q: What about UnionPay — do I need it?**
A: UnionPay is China's domestic card network. Most Chinese merchants only accept UnionPay, Alipay, or WeChat Pay. Your Mastercard/Visa card from outside China won't work at physical terminals here — that's why you link them through Alipay/WeChat Pay.

**Q: Can I use my Wise card directly at restaurants without Alipay?**
A: No. Chinese restaurants don't have POS terminals for foreign cards 99% of the time. You scan their QR code with Alipay or WeChat Pay. Always link your cards to these apps.

**Q: What's the minimum balance I should keep on Wise for a China trip?**
A: For two weeks, I'd load ¥6,000-10,000 equivalent on Wise. That covers food, transport, entrance fees, and a few DiDi rides. Hotels and flights are usually booked in advance.

**Q: What if my card gets declined and I have no cash?**
A: This is why you link BOTH cards to BOTH apps. If Wise fails on Alipay, try Wise on WeChat Pay. Or switch to Revolut on Alipay. Keep ¥500 in cash as your nuclear backup. This scenario has never happened to me — I'm just paranoid.

**Q: Do I need a Chinese phone number for any of this?**
A: Alipay and WeChat Pay both work with international numbers for registration and SMS verification. Just make sure your roaming supports incoming SMS in China.

**Q: Will my Wise/Revolut card work at Chinese hotel check-in?**
A: Most hotels require a cash deposit (¥300-500) for incidentals from foreign guests. Your Wise or Revolut card will work for this at your hotel's front desk POS terminal — but not always. Always carry ¥500-1,000 cash for hotel deposits.

**Q: What about tipping — do I need cash?**
A: Tipping is not customary in China. You'll never need cash for tips. Don't worry about it.
