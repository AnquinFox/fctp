# Chunyun 2027: How to Survive China's Spring Festival Travel Rush

![A packed railway station during the Spring Festival travel rush](/images/foreigners/china_chunyun_train_station_crowd_luggage_031.webp)

Chunyun. Forty days. Billions of trips. The largest annual migration of people on the planet.

If your trip overlaps late January to early March 2027, you're in it. Read this before you book.

---

## What is Chunyun, in one paragraph

Chunyun is the 40-day travel window around Chinese New Year. In 2027 that's roughly **January 22 – March 3**. Chinese New Year Day itself is **February 6, 2027**.

Everyone goes home for the holiday, then comes back. Trains, flights, buses and highways all run at maximum. Tickets sell out in seconds.

You don't have to avoid it. You do have to plan for it.

---

## When exactly is the crunch?

| Period | Dates (2027) | Crowd level |
|--------|--------------|-------------|
| Early rush | Jan 22 – Feb 2 | Heavy |
| **Peak outbound** | **Feb 3 – Feb 7** | Extreme |
| Holiday lull | Feb 8 – Feb 10 | Moderate (many stay put) |
| **Peak return** | **Feb 11 – Feb 16** | Extreme |
| Late rush | Feb 17 – Mar 3 | Heavy |

The two peaks are the problem. Everything in between is survivable.

---

## How early do tickets go on sale?

Roughly **15 days before departure**, on the official 12306 app.

That's it. Not months in advance. Fifteen days. So the plan is simple: know your date, and be on the app the moment the window opens.

Two things that actually help:

- **The waitlist (候补) system.** If a train is full, join the waitlist. It genuinely works — people cancel, and you get the seat. Don't pay a reseller for this; the official waitlist is free and usually faster.
- **Set a reminder.** The window opens at a fixed time in the morning. Miss it by an hour on a peak day and the good seats are gone.

![Booking a train ticket on a phone app](/images/foreigners/china_chunyun_phone_ticket_booking_app_032.webp)

---

## Book on 12306, not a reseller

Use the **official 12306 app** (English interface available) or a major platform like Trip.com.

Avoid:

- Third-party "ticket agents" that charge a markup and sometimes fail
- Anyone offering to "hold" a seat off-platform

You'll need your **passport number** — it must match your passport exactly. Bring the physical passport to the station; you'll scan it at the gate.

---

## Trains, flights, or something else?

| Option | Chunyun reality |
|--------|-----------------|
| **High-speed rail** | Best when it works. Sells out first. Book the second the window opens. |
| **Slow trains (K/T/Z)** | Often have seats when fast ones don't. Slower, but reliable. |
| **Flights** | More expensive in peak week, but sometimes the only option. Book 1–2 months out. |
| **Long-distance bus** | Crowded but flexible. Last resort. |
| **Didi / car-hire** | Good for short hops. Not practical for 1,000km. |

![A high-speed rail platform in China](/images/foreigners/china_chunyun_high_speed_rail_platform_026.webp)

**My rule:** if the train is hopeless for your date, fly. Don't burn three days waiting for a seat to open.

---

## Should I just avoid the peak?

If your dates are flexible: **yes, shift them.**

- Travel **Jan 15 – Jan 25** (before the crush, festive build-up) or
- Travel **Feb 20 – Mar 5** (after the crush, everything open again)

Skip Feb 3–7 and Feb 11–16 entirely if you can. It's the difference between a smooth trip and an airport floor nap.

---

## Getting around the city is fine

Here's the good news: chunyun is about **long-distance** travel. Inside a city, the metro, buses and taxis run normally — often quieter, because locals have left.

![Families using the metro with luggage](/images/foreigners/china_chunyun_metro_station_family_028.webp)

![A metro platform transfer during the holiday](/images/foreigners/china_chunyun_metro_platform_transfer_029.webp)

So Beijing's subway on Feb 6 is calm. It's the Beijing–Chengdu train that's a warzone.

---

## Practical survival tips

- **Arrive at the station 60–90 minutes early** on peak days. Security queues are long.
- **Carry your passport**, not a photo of it. You'll need it at the gate.
- **Don't trust your phone for storage** — bring a printed backup of your ticket and hotel address in Chinese.
- **Keep luggage light.** You'll be carrying it through crowds.
- **Charge everything** and bring a power bank. Station outlets are taken.
- **Watch your belongings** in the crush — pickpockets work the crowds.
- **Airport/station food is fine** but queues are long; eat before you go.

![Airport departure hall during the holiday rush](/images/foreigners/china_chunyun_airport_departure_hall_027.webp)

## What if I get stuck?

- **Join the 12306 waitlist** and keep checking — cancellations happen constantly.
- **Check the "next day" and "previous day"** options; flexibility beats stubbornness.
- **Look at nearby cities.** A train from a neighbouring hub can be wide open.
- **Fly the long leg**, then take a train for the short one.
- **Use a car-hire (包车)** for regional trips where trains are full — see below.

![Taxis and ride-hailing as a backup option](/images/foreigners/china_chunyun_taxi_didi_option_030.webp)

---

## Alternatives worth knowing

**Rent a car with a driver (包车).** For multi-city routes where trains are sold out, hiring a car + driver for the day is surprisingly affordable and fully flexible. Best for 2–4 people over 100–400km.

**Fly to a nearby hub.** Land in a secondary city and train/coach the rest of the way.

**Shift the whole trip.** Honestly, the single best "alternative" is just moving your dates by a week. See the [Spring Festival 2027 guide](/guides/foreigners/china-spring-festival-travel-guide/) for the full calendar.

---

## The one-line version

Book on 12306 the moment the 15-day window opens, shift your dates off the Feb 3–7 and Feb 11–16 peaks if you can, and remember: the city is fine — it's the long-distance leg that's the fight.
