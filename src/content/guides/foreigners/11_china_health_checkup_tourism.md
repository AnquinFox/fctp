# The $500 Full-Body Check-Up: The China Health-Screening Trip Nobody Talks About

> 📅 October 2026 · 🌏 For travelers who feel fine but want the full picture · ⏱️ 4–7 days on the ground

---

People ask me why I'd fly twelve hours to get poked and prodded. Fair question.

The answer is boring and simple. The comprehensive executive screening I was quoted **$8,000** for back home took me **half a morning** in Shanghai and cost about **$500**. I've done it three times now — once in Shanghai, once in Beijing, once in Chengdu with a big TCM detour attached — and I've sent maybe a dozen friends down the same path.

I'm not a doctor. This isn't medical advice, and I'm not going to tell you what to screen for or what any result means. What I *am* good at is the **logistics**: how to book it, what to send, what it costs, where to sleep, and how to turn a medical appointment into a genuinely great week in China.

Here's everything I've figured out.

## 💰 The Math, Because That's Why You're Here

Let me get the number that made me book a flight out of the way.

![Modern hospital exterior with glass facade in daylight](/images/foreigners/china_hospital_modern_exterior_facade_daylight_010.webp)

| What You Get | US Price | China Price | Notes |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **Comprehensive executive check-up** | ~$8,000 | **$500–800** (¥3,500–5,500) | Blood panel, ultrasound, ECG, chest CT, the works |
| Premium full-body (adds MRI + endoscopy) | $10,000+ | $1,100–2,100 (¥8,000–15,000) | Sleep in, results the next day |

| Low-dose chest CT | $1,200 | $120–200 | |
| Private single room + nurse | n/a | $60–150/night | Skip it unless you want the pampering |

◆ The gap isn't a trick. Chinese private and international hospitals do huge volume, there's no insurance middleman inflating the sticker, and a "hospital stay" here is often just a morning.

> **My rule of thumb:** a mid-tier international package plus a business-class flight plus a week of good hotels still lands *under* the US price of the test alone. The trip is the discount.

## 🏥 Which City Should You Actually Pick?

This is the question I get most, and the honest answer is: it depends on what you want the *rest of the week* to look like. The medicine is comparable across the big four — it's the trip around it that differs.

![Clean hospital lobby with reception desk](/images/foreigners/china_hospital_lobby_reception_desk_clean_011.webp)

| City | Best For | Hospital Type to Look For | The Vibe |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shanghai** | First-timers, easiest logistics | International departments of top public hospitals + JCI private clinics | English everywhere, walkable, great food, easiest to combine with sightseeing |
| **Beijing** | Most thorough screening, complex cases | Big flagship public hospitals' international wards | Mandarin capital, heavy hitters, add the Forbidden City |
| **Chengdu** | TCM + slow travel, best value | Provincial TCM hospital network + one international clinic | Teahouses, pandas, cheapest living of the four |
| **Sanya** | Recovery + beach, winter sun | Wellness/holiday clinics + Hainan's medical-pilot zone | Resort pace, warm sea, zero city stress |

Two trips I'd actually recommend:

- **First timer, one week:** Shanghai. Do the check-up on day two, spend the rest walking the Bund and eating your way through the old town.
- **Recovery-minded, two weeks:** Beijing or Chengdu for the screening, then fly south to Sanya for a slow beach week.

## 📝 How Do You Actually Book It?

The whole thing runs on email. You do it before you leave home.

![Doctor reviewing results with a patient on a tablet](/images/foreigners/china_doctor_consultation_results_tablet_patient_012.webp)

**Step 1 — Find the international department.** Search "(city) international hospital medical center" or "(city) hospital international patient department". Every big private hospital and most flagship public hospitals have an English-facing desk.

**Step 2 — Email them your basics.** A short message works:
- Your name, age, passport nationality
- What you want (e.g. "executive health check package, comprehensive")
- Any known conditions and current medications (so they can plan, not diagnose)

**Step 3 — Send your records.** PDFs of your last blood work, any scans, a one-page summary. English is fine — the international desks translate internally. Attach, don't paste.

**Step 4 — Get a written quote.** Good hospitals send back a package list with prices in ¥ and a rough USD figure. Ask what's *included* and what's optional. This is where you pick mid-tier vs premium.

**Step 5 — Pay your deposit and book a date.** Payment is usually a WeChat/Alipay link or an international card. Ask for the appointment confirmation in writing.

**Step 6 — Sort your visa.** A normal tourist visa covers this. If you're planning a longer stay or a procedure, the hospital can write an invitation letter.

> **Timing tip:** email 2–4 weeks ahead. Avoid late January to mid-February — Chinese New Year shuts down a lot of elective scheduling.

## 🩺 So What Does Check-Up Day Look Like?

Honestly? It's the calmest medical morning of my life.

![Hospital modern scan room](/images/foreigners/china_hospital_modern_exterior_facade_daylight_010.webp)

You arrive **fasting**, around 7:30–8:00am. You're walked through a circuit of stations — blood draw, weight and vitals, ultrasound, ECG, chest imaging — one after another, no waiting rooms full of forms. A staffer shadows you if you're the only non-Chinese speaker in the building. Total time: **half a day**, sometimes less.

Results come back fast — often **the same evening or next morning** — with a doctor sitting down to walk you through the report rather than sliding it under a door.

Then you're free by lunch. Which is the whole point: the appointment isn't the trip.

## 🌿 The TCM Add-On

This is the part you genuinely can't buy at home.

![Traditional Chinese medicine pharmacy with wooden herb drawers](/images/foreigners/china_tcm_herbal_pharmacy_wooden_drawers_013.webp)

Chengdu and Beijing are the two cities I'd choose for this. A proper session — acupuncture, or a consultation with a TCM doctor who actually reads your tongue and pulse for twenty minutes — runs about **¥300–500 ($40–70)**. A month of customized herbal medicine lands around **¥800 ($110)**.

◆ I'll say it plainly: I don't treat TCM as a replacement for anything. I treat it as a *"while I'm here"* add-on that costs less than dinner and is interesting in its own right. If it helps, great. If not, you've lost a coffee's worth of money compared to a Western specialist copay.

## 🌊 Turn It Into a Recovery Holiday

Here's the move locals in the know make: **don't fly home the day after your check-up.**

![Quiet tropical beach with palm trees at Sanya](/images/foreigners/sanya_yalong_bay_beach_resort_blue_sea_sand_007.webp)

Book a few days in **Sanya** (Hainan island, direct flights from every city above). Warm water, resort prices that look like a typo to anyone from California, and a genuine excuse to do nothing. Even if your results are clean, a couple of days on the beach after a medical morning is a better reward than a hotel breakfast.

If you're coming for anything more involved, this is where you take the *recovery* week — soft food, no rush, sun on your face.

## 🧳 Before You Fly: The Survival Kit

The check-up is easy. The *country* takes a little prep. Do these three things before you leave, and the trip runs itself:

1. **[Visa & entry](/guides/foreigners/visa-entry/)** — know your visa type and what to bring to the border.
2. **[Payment setup](/guides/foreigners/payment-setup/)** — get Alipay and WeChat Pay working *before* you land, or you'll be the person waving cash at a QR code.
3. **[Internet & VPN](/guides/foreigners/internet-vpn/)** — grab an eSIM and a working VPN before departure; you'll need Gmail to read your appointment emails.

Bring a paper copy of your passport and one printed page of your medical history. Chinese hospitals still love paper.

## ❓ Quick FAQ

**Is a health check-up trip legal / normal?**
Completely. China actively promotes medical travel, and international departments exist precisely to serve foreign patients.

**Do I need to speak Chinese?**
At international departments and JCI private hospitals — no. Doctors and front-desk staff handle English. Outside them, a translation app covers you.

**Can I use my travel insurance?**
For a check-up, usually no — it's elective. Pay cash (which is the point), and keep the English itemized invoice if you want to try claiming it at home.

**How long should I stay?**
Minimum 4 days: arrival, appointment, results, one fun day. I'd book a week every time.

**What should I avoid?**
Late January to mid-February (New Year), and any "international clinic" whose only credential is a translated website.
