# China Travel Budget 2026: Real Cost Breakdown

![Planning a China trip budget with yuan banknotes and a notebook](/images/foreigners/china_budget_trip_planning_notebook_yuan_085.webp)

Let me start with the number everyone actually wants: **a comfortable week in China costs roughly ¥5,000–7,500 (about $700–1,050) per person, excluding international flights.** A lean backpacker week can be done for ¥2,500. A polished, hotel-and-taxi week runs ¥12,000+.

The spread is enormous because China is genuinely cheap in some categories and surprisingly expensive in others. Hotels in Shanghai cost more than Berlin. A bowl of noodles in Chengdu costs $2. High-speed rail is world-class and priced accordingly. Once you know which line items swing, budgeting stops being guesswork.

This is the itemised version — real 2026 numbers, three spending tiers, four city examples, and a table you can copy into your own notes.

## The exchange rate I'm using

Currency moves, so pin it down before you read the numbers.

| Item | Assumption |
|------|------------|
| **Reference rate** | **USD 1 = ¥7.10** |
| **Plausible 2026 range** | ¥6.90 – ¥7.35 per USD |
| EUR | roughly ¥7.7 – ¥8.2 per EUR |
| GBP | roughly ¥8.9 – ¥9.5 per GBP |

> 💡 Every USD figure below is converted at **7.10**. If the rate moves 5%, your dollar costs move 5% — the yuan prices in this guide are the ones that don't change.

## Seven line items

![Withdrawing yuan from an ATM in China](/images/foreigners/china_budget_cash_atm_withdrawal_086.webp)

Here's what a China trip actually costs, and how much each line swings between tiers.

| Line item | Budget | Comfort | Premium |
|-----------|--------|---------|---------|
| **Visa** | $0 (visa-free) or ~$140 | ~$140 | ~$185 |
| **International flights** | $600–800 | $800–1,200 | $1,200–1,800 (premium econ) |
| **High-speed rail** | ¥100–200/leg | ¥300–550/leg | ¥550–1,800/leg (business) |
| **Accommodation** | ¥60–150/night | ¥350–650/night | ¥1,200–3,000/night |
| **Food** | ¥60–100/day | ¥150–300/day | ¥400–800/day |
| **Attractions** | ¥40–80/day | ¥100–200/day | ¥200–400/day |
| **Internet (eSIM/VPN)** | $5–15 total | $15–30 total | $30–50 total |

**The honest read:** flights and hotels are where the tier actually changes. Food and attractions compress far less than people expect — a great meal in Chengdu costs a fraction of a mediocre one in Shanghai, but the *gap between budget and premium eating* is smaller than the gap between hostel and five-star.

> 📌 **Visa note:** citizens of **50 countries** currently enter visa-free, which zeroes out this line entirely. Check your nationality in our [Visa & Entry guide](/guides/foreigners/visa-entry/) before budgeting $140 you may not owe.

## Three tiers: daily and 7-day totals

![Tracking travel expenses on a phone app](/images/foreigners/china_budget_expense_tracking_app_089.webp)

These are **on-the-ground** costs — accommodation, food, transport, attractions, incidentals. International flights are listed separately because they don't scale with your travel style.

### 🎒 Tier 1 — Budget / backpacker

| Category | Per day |
|----------|---------|
| Hostel dorm | ¥80 |
| Food (street + local canteens) | ¥80 |
| Metro + occasional bus | ¥20 |
| Attractions (one paid site/day) | ¥60 |
| Incidentals | ¥40 |
| **Daily total** | **≈ ¥280 (~$39)** |

**7 days: ≈ ¥1,960 (~$276)** + flights.

This is genuinely doable and not miserable — Chinese hostels are clean, street food is excellent, and the metro is cheap and fast. You're skipping taxis, paid attractions most days, and any restaurant with a printed menu.

### 🏨 Tier 2 — Comfort (the realistic default)

| Category | Per day |
|----------|---------|
| 3–4★ hotel, twin room | ¥480 |
| Food (mix of local restaurants + one good dinner) | ¥220 |
| Metro + 2–3 DiDi rides | ¥70 |
| Attractions (2 paid sites/day) | ¥150 |
| Incidentals | ¥80 |
| **Daily total** | **≈ ¥1,000 (~$141)** |

**7 days: ≈ ¥7,000 (~$986)** + flights.

This is where most independent travellers land: a proper hotel room, sit-down meals, taxis when it rains, and no agonising over a ¥120 ticket.

### ✨ Tier 3 — Light luxury

| Category | Per day |
|----------|---------|
| 5★ hotel or boutique | ¥1,800 |
| Food (good restaurants, wine) | ¥600 |
| Private car / DiDi premium | ¥200 |
| Attractions + guided experiences | ¥300 |
| Incidentals | ¥150 |
| **Daily total** | **≈ ¥3,050 (~$430)** |

**7 days: ≈ ¥21,350 (~$3,007)** + flights.

At this tier you're paying for space, service and time — not a fundamentally different China. The country doesn't get better, your logistics get smoother.

## City by city

![Ticket gate at a Chinese attraction](/images/foreigners/china_budget_attraction_ticket_entrance_087.webp)

Same country, four very different bills. These are **comfort-tier daily totals** for a solo traveller.

| City | Hotel (3–4★) | Food/day | Transport/day | Attractions/day | **Daily total** |
|------|--------------|----------|---------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| **Beijing** | ¥520 | ¥220 | ¥70 | ¥180 | **≈ ¥990** |
| **Shanghai** | ¥620 | ¥260 | ¥70 | ¥150 | **≈ ¥1,100** |
| **Chengdu** | ¥380 | ¥170 | ¥50 | ¥110 | **≈ ¥710** |
| **Xi'an** | ¥350 | ¥160 | ¥45 | ¥140 | **≈ ¥695** |

**What the table really says:**

- **Shanghai is the expensive one** — hotels run 20–30% above Beijing for equivalent quality.
- **Chengdu is the value champion.** Cheap hotels, transcendent food at street prices, and a compact centre.
- **Xi'an is cheap and dense** — the Terracotta Warriors and the city wall are the two big spends, everything else is walking distance.
- **Beijing sits in the middle**, but its attraction costs are the highest of the four (Forbidden City, Great Wall, Summer Palace all charge).

> 💡 **A 7-day two-city trip** — say Beijing + Xi'an at comfort tier — lands around **¥5,900 (~$830)** on the ground, before flights. That's the realistic middle of the market.

## Where the money leaks

![Activating an eSIM on a phone](/images/foreigners/china_budget_esim_phone_setup_088.webp)

Four places travellers consistently overspend, and how to plug them.

**1. Airport transfers.** A taxi from Pudong to central Shanghai is ¥180–250. The Maglev + metro combination is under ¥60 and often faster in traffic. Same story in Beijing.

**2. Buying rail tickets late.** Chinese rail pricing is fixed, but *availability* isn't — a sold-out second class forces you into first class at 1.6×. Book the moment the 15-day window opens; our [12306 booking guide](/guides/foreigners/china-12306-train-booking-guide/) walks through it.

**3. Connectivity.** Roaming data packs routinely cost $10/day. A travel **eSIM** costs $5–30 for the *whole trip* and activates before you land — see the [internet & eSIM guide](/guides/foreigners/internet-vpn/). This is the single easiest saving on the list.

**4. Booking hotels on arrival.** Walk-in rates in Shanghai and Beijing are brutal. Booking ahead through an OTA — and knowing which hotels legally accept foreign passports — saves both money and a wasted evening. Our [foreigner-friendly hotel guide](/guides/foreigners/china-hotels-accept-foreigners-guide/) covers the passport-registration rule that trips people up.

> 🛡️ **One line item worth not skimping:** travel insurance. A hospital visit without cover is the only cost on this page that can run to five figures. Compare policies before you fly — see the insurance section of our [health & emergency guide](/guides/foreigners/health-emergency/).

## FAQ

**Is China cheap or expensive?**
Both. Ground costs (food, transport, attractions) are low by Western standards. Hotels in tier-one cities and long-haul flights are not.

**How much for 7 days excluding flights?**
Budget ≈ ¥2,000 (~$280). Comfort ≈ ¥7,000 (~$990). Light luxury ≈ ¥21,000 (~$3,000).

**How much cash should I carry?**
¥300–500 as backup. Alipay and WeChat Pay cover almost everything — see [payment setup](/guides/foreigners/payment-setup/).

**Do I need to tip?**
No. Tipping isn't customary and can cause confusion.

**Are attraction tickets expensive?**
Major sites run ¥40–120. The Forbidden City is ¥60, the Terracotta Warriors ¥120, the Great Wall ¥40–45. Book popular ones ahead — some sell out.

**What's the cheapest month?**
Avoid Chinese New Year, National Day (early October) and summer holidays. Late spring and November are cheapest for hotels and flights.

**Can I budget without a Chinese bank account?**
Yes — a foreign card bound to Alipay or WeChat Pay works for most payments. See [payment setup](/guides/foreigners/payment-setup/).

## The Bottom Line

China's cost profile rewards planning more than most destinations. The transport and food are cheap; the hotels and flights are where your tier is decided. Pick your tier, add a 10% buffer, book rail the day the window opens, and buy an eSIM before you fly.

Then spend the savings on a second bowl of noodles. 🍜
