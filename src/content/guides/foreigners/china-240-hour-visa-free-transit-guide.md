# 240-Hour Visa-Free Transit in China: The Deep Dive Guide

![A modern Chinese airport transit terminal](/images/foreigners/china_transit_airport_layover_terminal_050.webp)

**240 hours. 10 days. 57 countries. 65 ports. No visa.**

This is the scheme that lets you turn a layover into a trip — or a trip into a longer one. It's also the one people get refused on most often, because the rules are precise and the exceptions are not obvious.

Everything below is verified against the National Immigration Administration (NIA) as of **October 2026**. Always re-check [nia.gov.cn](https://www.nia.gov.cn) before you fly.

---

## The Eligibility Card

| Field | Requirement |
|-------|-------------|
| **Who** | Citizens of **57 countries** |
| **Passport** | Valid, and meeting the onward-country entry rules |
| **Must have** | **Confirmed onward ticket to a THIRD country or region** |
| **Entry** | Any of **65 open ports across 24 provinces** |
| **Stay** | Up to **240 hours (10 days)** |
| **Allowed** | Tourism, business, visits, seeing family |
| **NOT allowed** | Work, study, journalism (these need a visa) |

## The Most Important Rule: The "Third Country"

![Boarding pass and onward ticket](/images/foreigners/china_transit_onward_ticket_boarding_pass_051.webp)

This is where most refusals happen.

Your onward ticket must go to a **third country or region** — meaning **not** the country you departed from, and **not** China.

| Route | Valid? |
|-------|--------|
| London → Beijing → Bangkok | ✅ Yes (third country) |
| New York → Shanghai → Tokyo | ✅ Yes |
| London → Beijing → London | ❌ No (returns to origin) |
| Hong Kong → Shanghai → Hong Kong | ❌ No |

**Also required:** the ticket must be **confirmed** — real, dated, with a seat. A "flexible" reservation or a screenshot isn't enough at the immigration desk.

---

## The 65 Ports (24 Provinces)

You can enter through any of the designated ports. The list is long, but these are the ones travellers use most:

- **Beijing** — Capital International / Daxing
- **Shanghai** — Pudong / Hongqiao
- **Guangzhou / Shenzhen** — Baiyun, Bao'an, and land crossings from Hong Kong
- **Chengdu, Xi'an, Kunming, Chongqing, Hangzhou, Nanjing, Wuhan, Xiamen, Qingdao**
- **Hainan** — Haikou and Sanya

**The catch:** you must stay **within the permitted area** for your entry port. Usually that's the province plus adjacent regions — but not always. Check the specific area for your port before planning a cross-country dash.

## The 24-Hour vs 240-Hour Question

| | 24-hour transit | 240-hour transit |
|---|---|---|
| **Ports** | **All** open ports | 65 designated ports |
| **Nationalities** | Anyone with an onward ticket | 57 countries |
| **Leave the port area?** | No — restricted area only | Yes, within the permitted region |
| **Need a visa?** | No | No |

If you just need to change planes, use the 24-hour rule — it's simpler and open to everyone. Use 240 hours only if you actually want to go sightseeing.

## Why People Get Refused

| Reason | Fix |
|--------|-----|
| Onward ticket goes back to the origin country | Rebook to a genuine third country |
| No confirmed onward ticket | Buy one and have it ready to show |
| Entering at a non-designated port | Check your port is on the list first |
| Planning to work / study | Apply for a proper visa |
| Overstaying the area or the 10 days | Set a hard reminder — overstays are penalised |
| Onward ticket doesn't exist yet | Book it *before* you fly |

> **My rule of thumb:** if a border officer can't read your intent from your documents in ten seconds, you'll get questions. Have the onward ticket and hotel booking printed or saved offline.

## Using It Well: The Layover Play

![Sightseeing during a long layover in China](/images/foreigners/china_transit_city_layover_tour_052.webp)

This is where 240-hour transit shines.

**A practical example:** fly London → Shanghai, spend four days seeing the Bund and Yu Garden, then fly Shanghai → Bangkok. One trip, two countries, no visa paperwork.

**Tips:**

- **Pick a port city with easy transit.** Shanghai, Beijing and Guangzhou have fast airport-to-city rail links.
- **Enter late, leave early** to maximise usable days — the clock starts the day after arrival.
- **Book the onward flight for the final day**, not before — you have 10 days to use.
- **Keep your hotel address in Chinese** — you'll need it at registration and possibly at immigration.
- **Don't over-plan** — the permitted area may restrict you to the province. Check first.

## Arriving: What Happens at the Desk

![Passport with an entry stamp at immigration](/images/foreigners/china_visa_free_entry_passport_stamp_044.webp)

![Immigration hall at a Chinese airport](/images/foreigners/china_visa_airport_immigration_hall_042.webp)

1. Join the **transit / visa-free** lane (ask staff if unsure)
2. Present your **passport**
3. Present your **confirmed onward ticket** to a third country
4. Answer where you're staying and how long
5. Fill in the **arrival card** — now available online before you fly
6. Register your accommodation **within 24 hours** (hotels do it automatically)

Keep your passport and a photo of your onward ticket with you at all times once you're in.

---

## The One-Line Summary

57 countries, 65 ports, 10 days — but only with a confirmed onward ticket to a **genuine third country**, entering at a designated port, and staying inside your permitted area.
