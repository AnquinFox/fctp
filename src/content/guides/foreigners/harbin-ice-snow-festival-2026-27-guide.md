# Harbin Ice and Snow Festival 2026–27: Dates, Tickets & How to Go (Edition Guide)

![Ice and Snow World glowing at night in Harbin](/images/foreigners/harbin_ice_snow_world_night_lights_festival_019.webp)

This is the one you book a year early. Harbin's Ice and Snow Festival is the biggest winter spectacle in China — an entire city of castles carved from river ice, lit from the inside, standing in −25°C. If you've seen photos and thought "that can't be real" — it's real. And it's colder and better than you imagine.

Here's the **2026–27 edition** guide: the exact window, what tickets cost, how to get there, and a 3-day plan. No fluff — go.

---

## 📅 The 2026–27 Edition — Dates & Tickets

**Ice and Snow World (冰雪大世界)** is the headline attraction. The 2026–27 iteration is expected to open in **late December 2026** and run through **late February 2027** (roughly **Dec 20, 2026 → Feb 28, 2027**). The official festival ceremony usually falls in **early January**.

| Item | Details |
|------|---------|
| 🗓️ **Window** | ~Dec 20, 2026 – Feb 28, 2027 |
| 📍 **Where** | Songbei District (松北区), north bank of the Songhua River |
| 🎫 **Ice World ticket** | ~¥330 peak / lower on weekdays — book online in advance |
| ⏰ **Best time to enter** | After 4pm — you want the lights on |
| 🌡️ **Temperature** | −18°C to −30°C (feels colder on the ice) |
| 👶 **Also nearby** | Sun Island Snow Sculpture Expo, Harbin Polarland |
| 🚇 **Getting there** | Metro Line 2 → Ice and Snow World station; or DiDi/taxi |

> ⚠️ **Peak trap:** the week of **Chinese New Year (Feb 5–11, 2027)** is the busiest and most expensive. If you can, come in **January** instead — same ice, fewer elbows.

## 🏰 Day 1 — Ice and Snow World + Songbei

### 🎡 游玩 (Play)

The main event. Block 4–5 hours, minimum. Enter in the late afternoon so you catch the ice glowing as the sun drops, then stay for the full light show.

Wear **two thermal layers** — it's 10 degrees colder out on the ice than downtown. Your phone will die; keep it against your body with a power bank.

![Ice castles glowing at night](/images/foreigners/harbin_ice_snow_world_night_lights_festival_019.webp)

### 🚇 交通 (Getting there)

Metro **Line 2** runs straight to Ice and Snow World station — busiest but cheapest and warmest option. DiDi/taxi from downtown is 25–40 minutes depending on traffic. Go early; the return taxi queue at night is brutal.

### 🍜 餐饮 (Food)

Eat **before** you go — food inside is limited and pricey. Back in the city, find a Dongbei BBQ joint for grilled lamb skewers and a bowl of hot noodle soup. You'll have earned it.

---

## 🏛️ Day 2 — Daoli District: Cathedrals & Central Street

### 🎡 游玩 (Play)

Morning: **Saint Sophia Cathedral** — a Byzantine onion dome against white snow, best around 8–9am before the crowds. Then walk **Central Street (中央大街)** — 1.4km of cobblestones, Art Nouveau and Baroque buildings, and street food the whole way.

![Saint Sophia Cathedral in the snow](/images/foreigners/harbin_sophia_cathedral_snow_gothic_020.webp)

### 🚇 交通 (Getting there)

Daoli is walkable — park the day here. Metro Line 2 to Central Street station, then on foot. It's genuinely a walking district.

### 🍜 餐饮 (Food)

Eat your way down Central Street: the **Modern Hotel ice pop** (yes, at −25°C), **Harbin red sausage (红肠)** from a street stall, and **Guo Bao Rou (锅包肉)** — crispy sweet-and-sour pork, the signature dish of the northeast.

---

## ❄️ Day 3 — Volga Manor, Temples & Snow

### 🎡 游玩 (Play)

**Volga Manor (伏尔加庄园)** — a Russian-themed park of wooden dachas and snow-covered bridges, about 30 minutes out. Dog sledding, horse sleighs, and a Russian sauna to warm up.

Back in town, the **Jile Temple** and **Confucius Temple** are quiet, snow-dusted and beautiful — the calm counterpoint to the ice spectacle.

![Volga Manor in the snow](/images/foreigners/harbin_volga_manor_russian_snow_033.webp)

### 🚇 交通 (Getting there)

Volga Manor needs a taxi or a booked transfer (30 min). The temples are in Nangang, reachable by metro plus a short walk.

### 🍜 餐饮 (Food)

Finish with a hot-pot or northern dumplings. And a bottle of **Harbin Beer** — brewed here since 1900.

## 🎒 What to Pack (Non-Negotiable)

![A snow-covered park in Harbin](/images/foreigners/harbin_snow_park_winter_frozen_034.webp)

- **Thermal base layer** top and bottom
- **Long, windproof down jacket**
- **Waterproof boots** rated for extreme cold
- **Thermal gloves + mittens**, ear-covering hat, scarf
- **Hand warmers** — buy a box, thank yourself later
- **Power bank** kept inside your jacket
- **Lip balm and moisturiser** — the dry cold cracks skin fast

---

## 🛠️ Practical Tips

- **Book Ice World tickets online** before you arrive — the queue at the gate in peak week is legendary.
- **Go in January** if you can avoid Chinese New Year.
- **Dress for −30°C**, not for "cold". Layering is everything.
- **Keep your phone warm** or it shuts down in minutes.
- **Cash and mobile payment both work** — set up Alipay before you fly (see our [payment guide](/guides/foreigners/payment-setup/)).
