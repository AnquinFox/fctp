# Skiing in China: Yabuli, Chongli & Changbaishan Compared

![Skiers on groomed powder slopes at a Chinese resort](/images/foreigners/ski_yaluli_powder_slopes_skiers_037.webp)

◆ A quieter truth about Chinese winter travel: the ice festivals get the crowds, but the skiing gets the horizon.

China is not the first country most travellers think of for a ski trip — which is precisely why the slopes are uncrowded, the lift passes are cheap, and the season runs long. From the Olympic pistes outside Beijing to the volcanic north, there is a resort for every level of ability and ambition. What follows is an editor's comparison of the three that matter most, plus what to expect on the ground.

---

## ◆ The Three Resorts That Matter

### 1. Chongli (崇礼) — The Olympic Option

![Alpine slopes and a gondola at Chongli ski resort](/images/foreigners/ski_chongli_alpine_olympic_slopes_038.webp)

The most accessible serious skiing in China. Chongli hosted the snow events of the **2022 Beijing Winter Olympics**, and the pistes and lifts have the modern, international feel you'd expect from a Games venue.

**Why it earns its place:** it is barely an hour from Beijing by high-speed rail — the easiest ski day trip from a major city anywhere in the country. Several resorts sit side by side (**Thaiwoo, Genting, Wanlong, Secret Garden**), each with its own character.

- **Best for:** weekend skiers, first-timers to China's slopes, anyone based in Beijing
- **Getting there:** ~1 hour by high-speed rail from Beijing North; resorts run shuttles
- **Editor's note:** book a specific resort, not "Chongli" — they're separate mountains

> **Verdict** — the pragmatic choice. Not the most dramatic scenery, but the smoothest logistics and the most consistent snowmaking.

---

### 2. Yabuli (亚布力) — The Big One

![A ski resort with pine forests and powder snow](/images/foreigners/ski_yaluli_powder_slopes_skiers_037.webp)

China's largest and most famous ski area, deep in Heilongjiang. Yabuli has hosted the Asian Winter Games and remains the benchmark for scale and terrain in the northeast. The season is the longest in the country — **November to April**.

- **Best for:** intermediate and advanced skiers, long trips, big mountain days
- **Getting there:** ~2.5 hours by train from Harbin — pair it with the [Ice Festival](/guides/foreigners/harbin-ice-snow-festival-2026-27-guide/)
- **Editor's note:** the resort is spread out; check whether your hotel is near your lifts

> **Verdict** — the destination. If you're flying to the northeast for the ice, the extra days here are worth it. Snow quality can rival the Alps on a good week.

---

### 3. Changbaishan (长白山) — The Scenic One

![Ski slopes beneath a snow-covered volcanic mountain](/images/foreigners/ski_changbaishan_resort_snow_mountain_039.webp)

The most beautiful of the three, set beneath a snow-capped volcanic peak on the North Korean border. Changbaishan pairs skiing with **hot springs** — a soak in steaming outdoor pools while snow falls is the signature experience.

- **Best for:** scenery lovers, couples, combined ski-and-spa trips
- **Getting there:** flights to Changbaishan Airport, then resort transfer
- **Editor's note:** the Heavenly Lake road can close in bad weather — have a plan B

> **Verdict** — the atmosphere. If you ski for the views as much as the turns, this is the one.

---

## ◆ Also Worth Knowing

- **Beidahu (北大湖)** and **Songhua Lake (松花湖)**, both in Jilin, are favourites among Chinese enthusiasts — quieter, cheaper, excellent snow.
- **Altay (阿勒泰), Xinjiang** is the region many researchers call the birthplace of skiing itself — for the adventurous and the altitude-tolerant.

## ◆ Before You Go: Equipment & Lessons

### Rent, don't pack

![Ski equipment rental shop with rows of skis and boots](/images/foreigners/ski_equipment_rental_skis_boots_041.webp)

Every major resort rents **skis, boots, poles, helmets and snowboards** on-site. Bringing your own gear is rarely worth the baggage fees unless you're an advanced skier with a specific setup.

Typical rental costs (per day):

| Item | Approx. price |
|------|---------------|
| Full ski set (skis + boots + poles) | ¥200–400 |
| Snowboard set | ¥200–400 |
| Helmet | ¥30–80 |
| Ski jacket / pants | ¥100–200 |
| Goggles | ¥50–100 |

Clothing rental is available but limited — **bring your own thermal layers** and a proper jacket. See the packing list in the [China Winter Guide](/guides/foreigners/china-winter-travel-guide/).

### Take a lesson — even if you "can ski"

![A ski instructor teaching a beginner on a gentle slope](/images/foreigners/ski_beginner_lesson_instructor_snow_040.webp)

Chinese resorts run **English-language ski schools** at the bigger mountains. For beginners, a half-day lesson on the nursery slopes is the difference between a great trip and an expensive tumble. Instructors are professional and reasonably priced by international standards.

## ◆ The Practical Column

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Season** | Late November – late March (northeast into April) |
| **Lift pass** | ~¥200–600 per day depending on resort and date |
| **Best month** | January — best snow, before the New Year crowds |
| **English support** | Good at Chongli and Changbaishan; limited at smaller resorts |
| **Payments** | Alipay / WeChat Pay everywhere — set up before you fly |
| **Altitude** | Not an issue — these are mountain resorts, not high plateau |

---

## ◆ The Closing Note

◆ Chongli rewards convenience. Yabuli rewards ambition. Changbaishan rewards the eye.

Whichever you choose, the calculus that makes China's skiing special is the same: world-class snow at a fraction of European or North American prices, on slopes that are still, for now, blissfully uncrowded. Go before the secret gets out.
