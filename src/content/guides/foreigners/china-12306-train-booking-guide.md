# China Train Travel: The 12306 Booking Guide for Foreigners

> 📅 Updated October 2026 · 📍 Built for foreign travellers · ⏱️ ~12 min read

![Registering on the 12306 train booking app with a passport on the desk](/images/foreigners/china_12306_app_registration_phone_065.webp)

◆ China's high-speed rail is the reason many travellers stop flying domestically. Trains run at 350 km/h, stations sit in the middle of cities rather than 40km outside, and the network reaches almost every provincial capital. The only obstacle is the booking system — and that obstacle is smaller than most people think.

This is the deep-dive guide to **12306**, the official railway booking platform: how to register as a foreigner, get your passport verified, catch the ticket release, pick the right seat, board with your passport, and handle refunds and rebookings without losing money.

> 🚆 For the wider picture — metro, taxis, flights, bike-share — see our [Transportation Guide](/guides/foreigners/transportation/). This article goes deep on trains only.

## Why book on 12306

There are three ways to buy a Chinese train ticket. Only one gives you the full inventory and the official price.

| Channel | Card | Passport needed | Notes |
|---------|------|-----------------|-------|
| **12306** (official) | Alipay / WeChat / Chinese bank card | ✅ | Full inventory, official price, waitlist access |
| **Trip.com / resellers** | International cards ✅ | ✅ | Convenient, small markup, no waitlist |
| **Station window** | Cash / card | ✅ | Works, but queues and language barriers |

**The honest trade-off:** 12306 is cheapest and most powerful, but needs **identity verification** before you can buy. Trip.com costs a little more and works immediately with a foreign card.

**My recommendation:** set up 12306 for the flexibility, and keep Trip.com as a fallback for when the app frustrates you.

## Step 1 — Register

![Photographing a passport for identity verification](/images/foreigners/china_12306_passport_identity_verification_066.webp)

Registration is genuinely foreigner-friendly now. You do **not** need a Chinese phone number.

1. Download the **12306** app (App Store / Google Play) or open the official site in a browser
2. Switch the language to **English** — the app has an English option under the "Me" tab
3. Tap **Register**
4. Choose **Scan to enter** to photograph your passport (the app reads it), or type the details manually
5. Enter an **email address** and set a password
6. Confirm the **verification email** sent to your inbox (check spam)

> 💡 **Name format matters.** Enter your name **exactly as printed in your passport, in English, in name order**. Middle names included. If the app shows an entry-format guide, follow it — mismatched names are the single most common cause of failed verification.

> 📌 **No Chinese number? No problem.** You can also bind WeChat to receive booking notifications without a mainland SIM.

## Step 2 — Identity verification

This is the step that stops most people, and it's simpler than it sounds.

After registering, your account sits at **"pending verification" (待核验)**. You cannot buy tickets until it clears.

**Two ways to clear it:**

| Method | How | Time |
|--------|-----|------|
| **Online** | Upload a **passport photo** and a **photo of yourself holding the passport** | Usually **3–5 working days** |
| **At a station** | Go to any station **ticket window** with your **passport original** | Same day |

**Practical notes from experience:**

- **Photo quality decides your wait.** A huge, high-resolution photo can fail the system. Crop it down to a small, clear image before uploading — that genuinely speeds up approval.
- **Everything must match the passport exactly** — name, name order, passport number, date of birth. One mismatched character and it bounces back.
- **If you're already in China**, the station-window route is faster and worth the trip.
- **You can still use Trip.com** while 12306 verification is pending. They're separate.

## Step 3 — Ticket release

![Selecting a seat class in the booking app](/images/foreigners/china_12306_seat_class_selection_screen_067.webp)

Here's the rule that surprises everyone: **Chinese train tickets are not sold months in advance.**

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **Presale window** | **15 days** before departure |
| **Booking cutoff** | Up to **30 minutes** before departure |
| **Online hours** | **05:00 – 01:00** (Tuesday: 05:00 – 24:00) |
| **Refunds** | Available **24 hours** a day |
| **Release time** | Varies by **departure station** — check the station's release time in the app |

**What this means in practice:** if your travel date is more than 15 days out, there is nothing to buy yet. Set a reminder for **the moment the window opens** on day 15 — on popular routes (Beijing–Shanghai, Guangzhou–Chengdu, anything into Sichuan), second-class seats can vanish within **minutes**.

> 🆕 **New: reservation booking (预约购票).** On pilot routes (including the Beijing–Shanghai corridor), you can now submit a **reservation 60 to 17 days ahead**, which the system fulfils **20 to 16 days ahead**. It's rolling out — check the app's "Reservation" section for your route.

> ⚠️ **Peak seasons break the rules.** Chinese New Year, National Day and summer holidays all see instant sell-outs. See our [Chunyun survival guide](/guides/foreigners/china-chunyun-travel-rush-survival-guide/) if you're travelling over Spring Festival.

## Step 4 — The waitlist

Once your identity is verified, you unlock the **waitlist (候补)** — the officially sanctioned way to get a sold-out ticket.

**How it works:**

- Order a waitlist spot on a sold-out train
- If anyone cancels, the system allocates the seat to waitlisted passengers in order
- You get a notification and a payment window

**What you can queue for:**

- Up to **3 travel dates** per order
- Up to **60 trains** in the queue
- A **different seat class per train**

> 🔑 **Why this beats a reseller:** it's free, it's official, and it's often faster than paying a middleman. The only requirement is verified identity.

## Step 5 — Train types and seats

![Interior of a modern high-speed train, second class](/images/foreigners/china_high_speed_train_second_class_interior_068.webp)

### Train prefixes

| Prefix | Type | Speed | Note |
|--------|------|-------|------|
| **G** | 高铁 (Gaotie) | 300–350 km/h | Fastest, most expensive |
| **D** | 动车 (Dongche) | 200–250 km/h | Slower, cheaper, still modern |
| **C** | 城际 (Chengji) | Intercity | Short regional hops |
| **Z / T / K** | Regular | 100–160 km/h | Sleeper trains for long overnight routes |

**Same route, different prefix, very different price.** A G-train and a D-train on the same corridor can differ by 30–40%. If you're not in a hurry, the D is excellent value.

### Seat classes (high-speed trains)

| Class | Chinese | Multiple | Best for |
|-------|---------|----------|----------|
| **Second Class** | 二等座 | 1× (base) | Most travellers — perfectly comfortable |
| **First Class** | 一等座 | ~1.6× | More legroom, 2+2 seating |
| **Business Class** | 商务座 | ~3× | Lie-flat, lounge access, premium |

**On regular trains (Z/T/K)** you'll also see sleepers: **soft sleeper (软卧)**, **hard sleeper (硬卧)**, and **hard seat (硬座)**. Soft sleeper gives you a four-berth compartment; hard sleeper is six berths in an open carriage. For overnight trips, book a sleeper — it's an experience and saves a hotel night.

> 💡 **Second class is not "cheap class" in China.** It's clean, spacious, has power sockets and a fold-down tray, and most Chinese travellers use it. First class is a comfort upgrade, not a necessity.

## Step 6 — Boarding

![Scanning a passport at the automatic gate in a Chinese railway station](/images/foreigners/china_railway_station_passport_gate_scan_069.webp)

China runs on **e-tickets** — no paper ticket needed in most cases.

**The standard flow:**

1. Arrive **45–60 minutes early** — security screening plus finding your gate
2. Go through **security** (bags scanned; liquids are fine on domestic rail)
3. Find your **waiting hall** and the gate shown on the departure board
4. At the gate, **scan your passport** on the reader
5. Board and find your seat

**Important for foreign passports:**

- **Most major stations read foreign passports at the gate.** If the reader won't accept yours, go to the **ticket window** and ask for a **paper ticket (取票)** — then use that at the gate.
- **Some smaller stations require paper tickets for foreign passports as standard.** If in doubt, collect one before the gate.
- **Gates close 5 minutes before departure.** They are strict. If you arrive late, you will not be let through.

![Ticket window and information counter at a Chinese railway station](/images/foreigners/china_railway_station_ticket_window_service_070.webp)

> 📌 **Keep your passport on you at all times inside the station.** It's your ticket, your ID and your boarding pass, all in one.

## Step 7 — Refunds and rebookings

### Refund fees (official ladder)

| When you cancel | Fee |
|-----------------|-----|
| **8 days or more** before departure | **Free** |
| **48 hours – 8 days** | **5%** of fare |
| **24 – 48 hours** | **10%** of fare |
| **Under 24 hours** | **20%** of fare |

Minimum fee is **¥2**. Refunds run 24 hours a day; booking runs 05:00–01:00.

### Rebooking rules

- **One rebooking per ticket.** That's it — no second change
- **A rebooked ticket cannot be refunded.** Choose carefully
- **More than 48 hours before departure:** rebook to any train within the presale period
- **Within 48 hours:** rebook only to a train before your original departure, or later the same day
- **After departure:** you can only rebook to another train the same day, at the departure station, and it cannot be refunded

> ⚠️ **Chunyun trap:** if your rebooked travel date falls **during Spring Festival**, a refund is charged at the **under-24-hours rate (20%)** regardless of when you cancel. Plan around it.

> 💰 **Get a receipt before you need it.** You can collect a reimbursement receipt (报销凭证) at the station within 180 days. But take it **after** you travel — collecting it beforehand blocks online refunds and rebookings.

## FAQ

**Can I register on 12306 without a Chinese phone number?**
Yes. Use an email address. You can also bind WeChat for notifications without a mainland SIM.

**How long does passport verification take?**
Online, usually 3–5 working days. At a station ticket window with your passport, same day.

**How far in advance can I buy?**
15 days. Not more — except on pilot routes with the new reservation service (60–17 days ahead).

**What if my train is sold out?**
Use the official waitlist (候补). You can queue up to 3 dates and 60 trains.

**Do I need a paper ticket?**
Usually not — your passport is the ticket. If the gate can't read your passport, collect a paper ticket at the window.

**Can I use my passport at the automatic gate?**
At most major stations, yes. At some smaller stations, collect a paper ticket first.

**What's the cheapest sensible option?**
A **D-train second class**. Almost as fast as a G, meaningfully cheaper.

**Can I get a refund on a rebooked ticket?**
No. Rebooking is final — a rebooked ticket can't be refunded.

**What if I miss my train?**
You can rebook same-day trains at the departure station, but the ticket can't be refunded.

## The Bottom Line

◆ 12306 is the cheapest and most capable way to buy Chinese train tickets — provided you do the one thing that takes patience: **verify your passport**. Once that's done, you have the full inventory, the official waitlist, and 15 days of presale to work with.

Register with an email, upload a small clean passport photo, wait a few days, then set an alarm for day 15. Everything after that is just choosing a seat and scanning your passport.
