# LASIK & ICL in China: The Logistics Guide (SMILE, Femtosecond, Cost & Recovery)

![Reception area of a modern eye centre in China](/images/foreigners/china_eye_center_modern_clinic_interior_060.webp)

I'll be honest: I didn't expect eye surgery to be a travel-planning topic. But once you see the price gap, you start doing the maths. In the US, laser vision correction runs into the thousands *per eye*. In China, the same modern platforms cost a fraction of that — and the clinics are, in the big cities, genuinely world-class.

This is a **logistics guide**, not medical advice. I'm not going to tell you which procedure is right for you — only a clinician can do that after examining your eyes. What I *can* do is walk you through what happens, what it costs, and how to plan a trip around it. 👓→😎

---

## 🏥 The Main Procedures (What They Actually Are)

![Laser vision correction suite](/images/foreigners/china_laser_vision_surgery_room_062.webp)

| Procedure | What it is | Typical profile |
|-----------|-----------|-----------------|
| **SMILE (全飞秒)** | Keyhole laser; a tiny 2mm incision, no flap | Popular for active people; fast recovery |
| **Femtosecond LASIK (半飞秒)** | A corneal flap is created, then laser reshaping | Most common laser type worldwide |
| **ICL (晶体植入)** | A thin lens implanted inside the eye | Often discussed for higher prescriptions or thinner corneas |
| **PRK / TransPRK (表层)** | Surface laser, no flap | Sometimes considered where flaps aren't suitable |

**The point of this table isn't to help you pick.** It's so you recognise the terms when a clinic sends you a quote in Chinese. 😌

> ⚠️ There is **no "best" procedure** — there's a suitable one determined by your corneal thickness, prescription, eye anatomy and other findings at screening. Clinics that tell you the answer before examining you are a red flag.

## 💰 The Cost Gap (2026, Both Eyes, RMB)

![Eye examination with diagnostic equipment](/images/foreigners/china_eye_exam_autorefractor_test_061.webp)

Prices are typically quoted for **both eyes** and usually **include pre-op screening, the procedure, medication and follow-up visits** — but you must confirm what's bundled. Different clinics bundle differently.

| Procedure | China (both eyes) | Rough US equivalent |
|-----------|-------------------|---------------------|
| Femtosecond LASIK | ¥10,000–20,000 | ~$4,000–6,000 |
| SMILE 3.0 | ¥15,000–25,000 | ~$5,000–6,000 |
| SMILE Pro | ¥20,000+ | — |
| ICL (domestic lens) | ¥20,000–30,000 | — |
| ICL (imported EVO) | ¥30,000–40,000+ | ~$8,000–10,000 |

**The takeaway:** typically **50–70% cheaper** than comparable Western pricing, even at the better private clinics in Shanghai, Beijing and Guangzhou.

> 💡 **Ask two questions before comparing quotes:** (1) Is this price for **both eyes**? (2) Are **screening, medication and follow-ups included**? A cheap headline number often excludes one or all three.

## 🗓️ The Process, Step by Step

### Step 1 — Pre-op screening (术前检查)

This is the longest part of the trip, and the part that decides everything.

- **Time needed:** 1.5–3 hours, in one visit
- **What it includes:** refraction, corneal topography, corneal thickness (pachymetry), eye pressure, dilated fundus exam, and more
- **The catch:** your pupils are **dilated** — bring sunglasses, and don't plan to drive afterwards. Vision is blurry for a few hours. 🌞

> 🔴 **Stop wearing contact lenses before screening.** Soft lenses: typically **1–2 weeks**. Rigid/RGP: **3–4 weeks**. If you don't, the measurements can be off and they may ask you to come back — which costs you days.

### Step 2 — The consultation

![Ophthalmologist discussing scan results with a patient](/images/foreigners/china_eye_doctor_consultation_patient_063.webp)

The doctor reviews your scans, discusses what's suitable, and quotes. Bring:

- Your **passport**
- A **translated summary** of your eye history if you have one
- Your **contact lens** prescription and habits
- Your **key questions written down** — consultations can be brisk

### Step 3 — The procedure

- **Duration:** typically 10–30 minutes for laser; longer for ICL
- Usually **one day**, day surgery — you go home the same day
- **Someone should accompany you**; you won't drive yourself back

### Step 4 — Post-op checks (复查)

The standard rhythm is roughly: **day 1 → 1 week → 1 month → 3 months.** Day 1 and week 1 matter most and are the ones to plan your trip around.

## ✈️ Can You Travel After? (The Planning Question)

![Eye centre reception with patients waiting](/images/foreigners/china_hospital_eye_center_reception_064.webp)

This is the bit nobody explains, so here it is straight.

| Question | Typical guidance |
|----------|------------------|
| **Can I fly afterwards?** | Often within a few days — but **ask your own surgeon**, as it depends on your case |
| **When can I return home?** | Most people plan around the **1-week check** before flying long-haul |
| **Can I swim / dive?** | Usually **not for 1–2 weeks**; follow your clinic's instructions |
| **Can I sightsee?** | Yes — but avoid dusty, smoky or very bright environments early on |
| **Can I rub my eyes?** | **No.** This is the single most-repeated post-op rule. 🙅 |

**A realistic trip shape:** arrive → screen (day 1–2) → procedure (day 2–4) → day-1 check → a few relaxed days → week-1 check → fly home. That's roughly **7–10 days** in China, which fits neatly inside a visa-free or tourist stay.

> 💡 If you're combining this with sightseeing, plan the sightseeing **after** the week-1 check, and keep it gentle — no sand, dust or pool water early on.

---

## 📋 Follow-Up After You Leave

If you go home before the 3-month check, sort this out **before you fly**:

- **Ask for your full records** — screening scans, procedure note, medication list — in a form you can hand to a doctor at home
- **Ask which checks your home optometrist can do**, and what the clinic wants to see
- **Get contact details** for the clinic's international department in case of questions
- **Carry your medication** in original packaging, with a doctor's note if you're flying

> 📌 If you're travelling on for the [medical visa route](/guides/foreigners/china-medical-visa-entry-guide/), or you're here on Hainan's scheme, make sure your allowed stay covers the day-1 **and** week-1 checks before you book anything.

## 🧳 Practical Checklist

- [ ] Stop contact lenses **1–2 weeks** (soft) or **3–4 weeks** (rigid) before screening
- [ ] Bring sunglasses for the dilated exam 🌞
- [ ] Confirm the quote covers **both eyes + screening + meds + follow-ups**
- [ ] Arrange a **companion** for procedure day
- [ ] Plan the trip around the **day-1 and week-1** checks
- [ ] Ask for **full records** before you fly home
- [ ] Set up **Alipay / WeChat Pay** — see [payment setup](/guides/foreigners/payment-setup/)

---

## 🎯 The Bottom Line

China offers **modern laser and ICL platforms at roughly half to a third of Western prices**, in well-equipped clinics in the major cities. The logistics are manageable: screen, treat, check, fly — about a week to ten days.

Two things to hold onto: **stop your contacts before screening**, and **don't rub your eyes afterwards**. Do that, and the trip takes care of itself.
